\section{\rev{Collaborative Optimal Transport under Bounded-Time LTL Tasks}}
\label{ch06:sec:ch06_stl}

\begin{revision}
Sections~\ref{ch06:sec:ch06_safe_plan} and~\ref{ch06:sec:ch06_uncertain}
address uncertain transition outcomes and moving-target forecasts,
respectively. Here the task and sampled motion model are given, and the
team must find trajectories that meet deadlines and geometric constraints
on visitation, formation, and connectivity.

Bounded-time LTL here denotes the fragment of metric temporal logic
with bounded future operators evaluated on a uniform sampling grid
\citep{koymans1990specifying}. As in Chapter~\ref{chap:preliminaries},
atomic propositions have Boolean truth values. Here those values come
from state predicates at each sample. For example, $\Diamond a$ expresses
eventual visitation, while $\mathsf{F}_{[0,h]}a$ requires a visit within
$h$ seconds. On the fixed grid, the latter is a disjunction of
$\bigcirc^j a$ over the sample offsets $j$ with $0\le j\Delta t\le h$.
In this expansion each next operator advances one sample
\citep{alsmann2025metric}.

\citet{maity2015motion} augment the spatial abstraction with time so
that standard LTL planning can express bounded-time requirements.
Here the optimizer uses real-valued predicate margins to compute the
robustness surrogate that guides the search. For final verification, a
finite automaton tests whether exact task robustness is strictly positive.
The robustness recursion
follows quantitative metric and signal temporal logic
\citep{fainekos2009robustness,donze2010robust}.
The method combines Gaussian-process trajectory priors, batched
robustness evaluation, sequential local optimal transport, and a hybrid
search over planning orders.
\end{revision}

\subsection{\rev{Multi-robot motion planning under bounded-time LTL tasks}}
\label{ch06:subsec:problem_formulation}

%==============================
\subsubsection{Multi-robot system under dynamic constraints}
\label{ch06:sec:multi-robot-system}

Consider a system of $N$ robots indexed by
$\mathcal{N} \triangleq \{1,\cdots,N\}$ and evolving in a workspace
$\mathcal{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^D$, where $D\geq 2$.
Each robot $i\in\mathcal{N}$ follows the discrete-time first-order dynamics below:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:robot-model}
\mathbf{p}_i(t+1)=\mathbf{p}_i(t)+\mathbf{v}_i(t)\Delta t,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_i(t),\mathbf{v}_i(t)\in\mathbb{R}^D$ denote,
respectively, the position and velocity of robot $i$ at time
$t\in\{0,\cdots,T\}$, and $\Delta t>0$ is the sampling period.
Each robot occupies a physical region $\mathcal{A}_i(t)$ at time $t$,
for instance a disk centered at $\mathbf{p}_i(t)$.
The full state of robot $i$ is defined by:
$\mathbf{q}_i(t)\triangleq (\mathbf{p}_i(t),\mathbf{v}_i(t))$.
For the team-level description, let
$\mathbf{x}(t)\triangleq [\mathbf{p}_1(t),\cdots,\mathbf{p}_N(t)]$ and
$\mathbf{u}(t)\triangleq [\mathbf{v}_1(t),\cdots,\mathbf{v}_N(t)]$
be the stacked position and control vectors, respectively.
The collective state is then
$\mathbf{z}(t)\triangleq (\mathbf{x}(t),\mathbf{u}(t))$.
\rev{With terminal sample index $T$, the system trajectory has $T+1$ samples
$\mathbf{Z}(T)\triangleq [\mathbf{z}(0),\cdots,\mathbf{z}(T)]^\intercal$,
and $\mathbf{Z}_i(T)$ denotes the corresponding trajectory of robot $i$.}
The workspace also contains static obstacles represented by
$\mathcal{O}\subset\mathcal{W}$.

The control effort and trajectory smoothness of robot $i$ are measured by a
stage cost
$h_i:\mathbb{R}^{2D}\times\mathbb{R}^{2D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^+$.
Using $h_i$, the cumulative control cost over $\mathbf{Z}_i(T)$ is defined by
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:control-cost}
c_{\texttt{ctr}}\big(\mathbf{Z}_i(T)\big)
\triangleq \sum_{t=0}^{T-1}
h_i\big(\mathbf{q}_i(t),\mathbf{q}_i(t+1)\big),
\end{equation}
where the function $h_i$ may differ across robots to capture
heterogeneous motion characteristics.
In addition to \eqref{ch06:eq:control-cost}, the collective trajectory is
penalized by state-dependent costs associated with obstacle avoidance,
inter-robot separation, and task satisfaction.
\rev{For obstacle avoidance, define
$g_{\texttt{obs}}\big(\mathbf{p}_i(t)\big)\triangleq
\max\{0,r_{\texttt{o}}-\texttt{MSD}(\mathcal{A}_i(t),\mathcal{O})\}$,
where $r_{\texttt{o}}>0$ is a safety margin and
$\texttt{MSD}(\cdot,\cdot)$ is the minimum signed distance between two
manifolds~\citep{schulman2014motion}.}
For inter-robot collision avoidance, define
$g_{\texttt{int}}\big(\mathbf{p}_i(t),\mathbf{p}_j(t)\big)\triangleq
\max\{0,r_{\texttt{s}}-
\texttt{MSD}(\mathcal{A}_i(t),\mathcal{A}_j(t))\}$,
where $r_{\texttt{s}}>0$ is the required pairwise safety margin.
Both penalties vanish when the corresponding clearance requirement is met.


%==============================
\subsubsection{\rev{Bounded-time LTL and trajectory robustness}}
\label{ch06:subsubsec:stl_robustness}

\begin{revision}
Let $\mathbf{s}:\{0,\cdots,T\}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}$ be a sampled state
signal with sampling period $\Delta t$. Each atom $\mu$ is a Boolean
predicate on $\mathcal{X}$ with an associated real-valued margin
$f_\mu:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$. Its truth value is obtained by
testing that margin against zero, using the predicate's specified strict
or non-strict inequality. The bounded-time LTL formulas are generated by
$\phi ::= \top \mid \mu \mid \lnot \phi \mid \phi_1 \wedge \phi_2
\mid \phi_1 \vee \phi_2 \mid \phi_1 \mathsf{U}_I \phi_2$,
where $I$ is a closed bounded interval of nonnegative offsets in seconds.
For a sample index $t$, define $t\oplus I\triangleq
\{j\ge t\mid (j-t)\Delta t\in I\}$, with integer $j$.
The horizon is chosen to contain the samples required by the formula,
including nested time windows.
The formula $\phi_1\mathsf{U}_I\phi_2$ holds at $t$ if $\phi_2$ holds
at some $j\in t\oplus I$ and $\phi_1$ holds at every sample from $t$
through $j-1$. The operators $\mathsf{F}_I$ and $\mathsf{G}_I$ are
derived from $\mathsf{U}_I$ as in the untimed case.
Satisfaction at sample $t$ is denoted by $(\mathbf{s},t)\models\phi$.
The trajectory objective uses the spatial robustness
$\rho^\phi(\mathbf{s},t)$ with the recursion
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:stl-robustness}
\begin{aligned}
\rho^\top(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq +\infty, \\
\rho^\mu(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq f_\mu(\mathbf{s}(t)), \\
\rho^{\lnot\phi}(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq -\rho^\phi(\mathbf{s},t), \\
\rho^{\phi_1\wedge\phi_2}(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq \min\big\{
\rho^{\phi_1}(\mathbf{s},t),\rho^{\phi_2}(\mathbf{s},t)\big\}, \\
\rho^{\phi_1\vee\phi_2}(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq \max\big\{
\rho^{\phi_1}(\mathbf{s},t),\rho^{\phi_2}(\mathbf{s},t)\big\}, \\
\rho^{\mathsf{G}_I\phi}(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq \min_{t'\in t\oplus I}\rho^\phi(\mathbf{s},t'), \\
\rho^{\mathsf{F}_I\phi}(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq \max_{t'\in t\oplus I}\rho^\phi(\mathbf{s},t'), \\
\rho^{\phi_1\mathsf{U}_I\phi_2}(\mathbf{s},t)
&\triangleq \max_{t'\in t\oplus I}
\min\Big\{\rho^{\phi_2}(\mathbf{s},t'),
\min_{t''\in [t,t')}\rho^{\phi_1}(\mathbf{s},t'')\Big\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where all extrema range over integer indices and
$\min\varnothing=+\infty$ and $\max\varnothing=-\infty$.
Positive robustness implies Boolean satisfaction, and negative robustness
implies violation. At zero robustness, the Boolean predicate conventions
determine satisfaction~\citep{fainekos2009robustness,donze2010robust}.
\end{revision}


\rev{The task-related term is defined through trajectory robustness.}
Two classes of atomic predicates are used.
(I) \emph{Regional predicates}.
Given a region $\mathcal{W}_k\subset\mathcal{W}$, let
$\mu_A^k\triangleq
-\max_{i=1,\cdots,N}\big(
\texttt{MSD}(\mathcal{A}_i(t),\mathcal{W}_k)+2r\big)\geq 0$,
where $r$ is the robot radius.
The predicate $\mu_A^k$ is satisfied when all robots lie inside
$\mathcal{W}_k$ at time $t$.
(II) \emph{Collaborative predicates}.
\rev{The predicate $\mu_B^k$ thresholds the margin
$\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{z}(t))$ at zero according to the team-level requirement.}
Two representative collaborative constraints are considered.
(I) The first is \emph{relative formation}, described by
$\mathcal{F}_{\texttt{form}}\big(\mathbf{z}(t)\big)\triangleq
\epsilon_{\texttt{f}}-\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}}
f_{ij}(\mathbf{q}_i,\mathbf{q}_j)$,
where $\epsilon_{\texttt{f}}>0$ is the maximum admissible formation error.
\rev{The formation predicate holds when this margin is nonnegative.}
The term $f_{ij}$ quantifies the formation mismatch between neighboring
robots $(i,j)\in\mathcal{E}$.
Typical choices include
$f_{ij}\triangleq \big|\|\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j\|-d_{ij}\big|$
for distance-based formations~\citep{antonelli2014decentralized} and
$f_{ij}\triangleq
\|(\mathbf{p}_i-\mathbf{p}_j)-\overline{\mathbf{p}}_{ij}\|$
for relative-pose formations~\citep{alonso2017multi}.
(II) The second is \emph{connectivity maintenance}, described by
$\mathcal{F}_{\texttt{con}}\big(\mathbf{z}(t)\big)\triangleq
\lambda_2\big(L(t)\big)$,
where $L(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}$ is the Laplacian matrix of the
communication graph $\mathcal{G}_{\texttt{c}}(t)$.
\rev{The connectivity predicate holds exactly when
$\lambda_2\big(L(t)\big)>0$~\citep{sabattini2013distributed}.}
Unlike pairwise spacing constraints, this condition is a system-level
property.

\rev{A collaborative task is a bounded-time LTL formula $\phi$ over the
regional and collaborative predicates in $\mu\triangleq \{\mu_A^k,\mu_B^k\}$.}
Its trajectory-level penalty is evaluated through the robustness
$\rho^\phi(\mathbf{Z}(T),0)$ together with the geometric costs defined
above.
The resulting state-dependent cost is given by:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:state-cost}
\begin{aligned}
c_{\texttt{tsk}}\big(\mathbf{Z}(T)\big)\triangleq
&-\rho^\phi\big(\mathbf{Z}(T),0\big) \\
&+\sum_{t=0}^{T}
\Bigg(
\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}} g_{\texttt{obs}}\big(\mathbf{p}_i(t)\big)
+\sum_{i<j\in\mathcal{N}}
g_{\texttt{int}}\big(\mathbf{p}_i(t),\mathbf{p}_j(t)\big)
\Bigg),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\rev{where the first term promotes timed task satisfaction, while the remaining
terms penalize obstacle proximity and inter-robot collisions.}
\begin{revision}
Before optimization, constants and empty reductions are folded into the
expanded formula tree using the exact min and max rules. If the root reduces
to true, the task robustness term contributes zero to every optimization cost.
If it reduces to false, the sampled task is infeasible and the search returns
failure. All remaining numerical leaf margins and Sinkhorn costs are finite.
\end{revision}
Hence, minimizing \eqref{ch06:eq:state-cost} encourages both safety and
task fulfillment.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Collaborative motion planning problem}

\rev{Given initial and target states
$\mathbf{x}_{\texttt{s}},\mathbf{x}_{\texttt{g}}\in\mathbb{R}^{DN}$ and an
associated bounded-time LTL task $\phi$, the collaborative motion planning
problem is formulated as follows.}

\begin{problem}
\label{ch06:prob:multi-robot-control}
Find a system trajectory $\mathbf{Z}(T)$ that minimizes the sum of the
task-dependent cost in \eqref{ch06:eq:state-cost} and the individual control
costs in \eqref{ch06:eq:control-cost}, subject to the boundary conditions and
the dynamics in \eqref{ch06:eq:robot-model}, i.e.,
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:prob}
\begin{aligned}
\underset{\mathbf{Z}(T)}{\textbf{min}}\quad
& c_{\texttt{tsk}}\big(\mathbf{Z}(T)\big)
+ \sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}} c_{\texttt{ctr}}\big(\mathbf{Z}_i(T)\big), \\
\textbf{s.t.}\quad
& \mathbf{x}(0)=\mathbf{x}_{\texttt{s}}, \mathbf{x}(T)=\mathbf{x}_{\texttt{g}}, \\
& \mathbf{p}_i(t+1)=\mathbf{p}_i(t)+\mathbf{v}_i(t)\Delta t,
\quad \forall i\in\mathcal{N},\ \forall t\in\{0,\cdots,T-1\},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the optimization variable is the collective trajectory
$\mathbf{Z}(T)$ over the horizon $T$.
\end{problem}

\begin{revision}
Bounded temporal operators introduce time-indexed conjunctions and
disjunctions into Problem~\ref{ch06:prob:multi-robot-control}.
\citet{wolff2014control} shows how LTL constraints can be encoded
with mixed-integer linear constraints on trajectory variables.
Related mixed-integer formulations for timed specifications use logical
variables to represent choices among samples and subformulas
\citep{sun2022multiagent,kurtz2022mixed}. The transport updates use a
robustness surrogate to generate candidate trajectories. In final verification,
the finite automaton checks whether a complete candidate has strictly positive
exact task robustness.
\end{revision}

\subsection{\rev{Batched evaluation of perturbed robustness}}
\label{ch06:subsec:batch-stl}

The Sinkhorn update requires efficient batch evaluation of robustness changes
caused by perturbing one waypoint along multiple probing directions.

%%===================
\subsubsection{\rev{Formula tree}}
\label{ch06:subsec:stl-tree}

\begin{revision}
A bounded-time LTL formula $\phi$ can be decomposed recursively into
subformulas with Boolean and temporal operators
\citep{fainekos2009robustness,kurtz2022mixed,leung2023backpropagation}.
The formula tree represents this structure as
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:stl-tree}
\mathcal{T}^{\phi}\triangleq
\big(\widehat{\mathcal{T}},\mathcal{I},\circ\big),
\end{equation}
where
$\widehat{\mathcal{T}}\triangleq
[\mathcal{T}^{\phi_1},\cdots,\mathcal{T}^{\phi_K}]$
is the list of $K\geq 1$ child subtrees,
$\mathcal{I}\triangleq [I^{\phi_1},\cdots,I^{\phi_K}]$
collects the associated time intervals, and
$\circ\in\{\wedge,\vee\}$ specifies the Boolean composition at the
current node.
\end{revision}
Leaf nodes correspond to predicates evaluated at particular time
instants, whereas internal nodes aggregate the child robustness values.
In particular, the temporal operators $\mathsf{F}_I$ and
$\mathsf{G}_I$ are realized through disjunctive and conjunctive
compositions, respectively, while $\mathsf{U}_I$ induces a recursive
combination of both.
\rev{After expanding the bounded temporal operators over the samples,
negations are pushed to the leaves using De Morgan's laws and evaluated
by reversing the sign of the corresponding margins.}
The representation in \eqref{ch06:eq:stl-tree} enables bottom-up
robustness evaluation over the formula tree.

\subsubsection{\rev{Finite automaton for exact robustness}}
\label{ch06:subsubsec:robustness-automaton}
\begin{revision}
Expand $\phi$ at the initial sample using the windows $t\oplus I$,
push negations to the leaves, and fold constants. Each remaining leaf
is a time-tagged literal. A literal $\ell$ is a predicate $\mu$ or its
negation, with margin $m_\ell(j)=f_\mu(\mathbf{s}(j))$ or
$m_\ell(j)=-f_\mu(\mathbf{s}(j))$, respectively. Let $\mathcal{L}_\phi$
contain these signed literals. At threshold $\lambda$, the letter
at sample $j$ is $\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_\phi\mid m_\ell(j)\geq\lambda\}$.

The automaton $\mathcal{A}_\phi$ reads one letter per sample. Its state
records the next sample index and the remaining Boolean formula.
At sample $j$, it replaces the leaves tagged with $j$ by their truth
values from the letter and simplifies the result. The finite grid and
the finite set of literal valuations give finitely many reachable
residual formulas. It accepts if the
residual is true after all $T+1$ samples. The formula and sample grid
fix the transitions, so the same automaton can be reused at different
thresholds and across candidate trajectories.

Thresholding a minimum gives a conjunction, and thresholding a maximum
gives a disjunction. Applying these identities to the expanded tree shows
that the automaton accepts exactly when $\rho^\phi(\mathbf{s},0)\geq\lambda$.
For a nonconstant tree this robustness is the largest accepted threshold
among its distinct leaf margins, which can be found by binary search.
Under \eqref{ch06:eq:stl-robustness}, a tree that reduces to true has
robustness $+\infty$, and one that reduces to false has robustness $-\infty$.
In the optimization, the task robustness term is set to zero for a true root,
and a false root causes the search to return failure, as specified after
\eqref{ch06:eq:state-cost}.
A single pass with strictly positive margins in the letters tests
$\rho^\phi(\mathbf{s},0)>0$ for final verification.
\end{revision}

%%===================
\subsubsection{Batched evaluation of perturbed robustness}
\label{ch06:subsec:stl-robustness}

\rev{The robustness recursion evaluates predicate margins at the leaves of
$\mathcal{T}^{\phi}$ and propagates $\min$ and $\max$ through conjunction and
disjunction nodes.} For perturbation-based optimization, this rule can be
locally insensitive: if the perturbed child of a conjunction is not the
minimizer, its effect is masked. A surrogate perturbed robustness is therefore
used to retain sensitivity while preserving the formula tree.
Let $\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}_{t^\star}(T)$ denote a trajectory obtained by
perturbing only the $t^\star$-th waypoint of $\mathbf{Z}(T)$.
For a fixed time index $t^\star$, let
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}}_{t^\star}\triangleq
[\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}_{t^\star}^{(1)}(T),\cdots,
\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}_{t^\star}^{(M)}(T)]$
be the batch of perturbed trajectories generated along the $M$ probing
directions used by the local transport step.
The predicate values at the leaf nodes are first computed in parallel
for all elements of
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}}_{t^\star}$.
\begin{revision}
The perturbation mask uses the same sample indices as the exact semantics.
At a parent sample $t$, a window $I$ contributes the indices $t\oplus I$.
Nested windows use the sample of their own enclosing operator. Thus a
leaf is affected precisely when its assigned sample index equals $t^\star$.
At a conjunction directly above the leaves, the surrogate takes the minimum
over the affected children, or over all children if none are affected.
Other conjunctions use the mean of their child values, and disjunctions
use the maximum.
\end{revision}
Accordingly, the perturbed robustness of a single perturbed trajectory is
defined as:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:perturbed-robustness}
\widehat{\rho}^{\phi}\big(\widehat{\mathbf{Z}}_{t^\star}(T)\big)
\triangleq
\texttt{Eval}_{\mathcal{T}^{\phi}}
\Big(
\big\{f_i^k\big(\widehat{\mathbf{z}}(t)\big)\big\},
\{\max,\min,\operatorname{mean}\}
\Big),
\end{equation}
where $\widehat{\mathbf{z}}(t)$ is the state of the perturbed trajectory
at time $t$, and $f_i^k$ denotes the predicate-robustness function
associated with the corresponding leaf node.
The same recursion is then applied elementwise to the batch
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Z}}}_{t^\star}$, so that the perturbed
robustness values for all probing directions are obtained in a single
bottom-up traversal.
The construction preserves the tree-based formula interpretation, improves
local sensitivity, and supports batched computation. \rev{The surrogate in
\eqref{ch06:eq:perturbed-robustness} guides local updates, and
$\mathcal{A}_\phi$ checks positive exact robustness for the final trajectories.}

\subsection{Sequential local optimal transport}
\label{ch06:sec:local}

Using the batched robustness evaluation, the multi-robot problem is decomposed
into lower-dimensional subproblems. Each stage optimizes one robot trajectory,
treating previously planned trajectories as time-dependent constraints. This
reduces local dimension, but the final solution depends on the planning order
because early trajectories restrict later feasible and low-cost regions.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Design of planning sequence}
\label{ch06:subsec:sequence-design}

To encode the couplings that must be respected during sequential
planning, introduce an undirected dependency graph
$\overline{\mathcal{G}}\triangleq
(\mathcal{N},\overline{\mathcal{E}})$,
where $\overline{\mathcal{E}}$ represents the pairwise constraints that
link robot trajectories.
Global requirements, such as connectivity maintenance, are represented
through the pairwise dependencies induced by the initial communication
graph.
For each robot $i\in\mathcal{N}$, define the neighbor set
$\mathcal{N}_i^{\overline{\mathcal{E}}}\triangleq
\{j\in\mathcal{N}\mid (i,j)\in\overline{\mathcal{E}},\ i\neq j\}$.
The planning sequence is written as below:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:order}
\kappa\triangleq [i_1,\cdots,i_N],
\end{equation}
where $i_\ell\in\mathcal{N}$ for each
$\ell\in\{1,\cdots,N\}$.
\rev{For every stage $\ell\geq 1$, let
$\mathcal{N}_\ell^-\triangleq \{i_1,\cdots,i_{\ell-1}\}$ denote the set
of robots planned before $i_\ell$, with $\mathcal{N}_1^-=\varnothing$.}
The sequence $\kappa$ is called valid if every robot $i_\ell$ with
$\ell>1$ is constrained by at least one preceding robot, namely,
\[
i_\ell\in
\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{N}_\ell^-}\mathcal{N}_j^{\overline{\mathcal{E}}},
\]
\rev{where the membership condition requires a dependency on at least one
previously planned robot.}
Moreover, for time-varying pairwise constraints, let
$\overline{\mathcal{G}}_s\triangleq
(\mathcal{N},\overline{\mathcal{E}}_s)$,
$s\in\{1,\cdots,S\}$, denote the dependency graphs associated with the
different time indices or operating modes.
The validity condition is then strengthened as follows:
\[
i_\ell\in
\bigcap_{s=1}^S
\Big(
\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{N}_\ell^-}
\mathcal{N}_j^{\overline{\mathcal{E}}_s}
\Big),
\]
\rev{where the intersection requires such a dependency in every graph}
$\overline{\mathcal G}_s$.
Hence, valid sequences support feasible sequential decompositions,
whereas arbitrary orderings can easily produce incompatible local
subproblems.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Constrained trajectory optimization for each robot}
\label{ch06:subsec:sequential-planning}

Fix a valid planning sequence
$\kappa=[i_1,\cdots,i_N]$.
The sequential procedure solves one local trajectory optimization problem
for each robot in the order prescribed by \eqref{ch06:eq:order}.
\begin{revision}
For each stage $\ell\in\{1,\cdots,N\}$, the previously planned trajectory set is
\[
\Gamma_\ell^-\triangleq
\{\mathbf{Z}_{i_1}^\star(T),\cdots,
\mathbf{Z}_{i_{\ell-1}}^\star(T)\},
\]
where these trajectories remain fixed while optimizing robot $i_\ell$. Let
$\Gamma_\ell\triangleq
\Gamma_\ell^-\cup\{\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}(T)\}$
be the partial plan after adding the candidate trajectory of robot $i_\ell$.
During local optimization, each robot outside this partial plan is
represented by the mean trajectory of its Gaussian-process prior
conditioned on its endpoints. These means are fixed across the probes.
The resulting full team signal, denoted by $\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{p}}_\ell(T)$,
is used to evaluate surrogate task robustness during the search. The local
state-dependent cost for robot $i_\ell$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:state-cost-sequential}
\begin{aligned}
c_{\texttt{tsk}}^{i_\ell}\big(\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}(T)\big)
\triangleq
&\sum_{t=0}^{T}
\Bigg(
\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_\ell^-}
g_{\texttt{int}}
\big(\mathbf{p}_{i_\ell}(t),\mathbf{p}_{j}(t)\big)
+ g_{\texttt{obs}}\big(\mathbf{p}_{i_\ell}(t)\big)
\Bigg)-\widehat{\rho}^{\phi}\big(\mathbf{Z}^{\mathrm{p}}_\ell(T)\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the clearance terms use the preceding trajectories and the obstacles,
and the last term scores the full team signal. At the first stage the sum
over preceding robots is empty. At the last stage every robot trajectory
in the signal comes from the partial plan enlarged by the current candidate.
\end{revision}
Combining \eqref{ch06:eq:state-cost-sequential} with the control cost in
\eqref{ch06:eq:control-cost}, the local optimization problem for robot
$i_\ell$ is written as:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:local-problem}
\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^\star(T)\triangleq
\underset{\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}(T)}{\textbf{argmin}}
\Big\{
c_{\texttt{tsk}}^{i_\ell}\big(\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}(T)\big)
+
c_{\texttt{ctr}}\big(\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}(T)\big)
\Big\},
\end{equation}
where the optimization is subject to the robot dynamics in
\eqref{ch06:eq:robot-model} and to the boundary conditions inherited from the
global problem.
After solving \eqref{ch06:eq:local-problem}, the resulting trajectory
$\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^\star(T)$ is appended to the collective set and is
used in the optimization of the subsequent robots.

%==============================
\begin{algorithm}[t!]
  \caption{Local Sinkhorn update for a single robot.}
  \label{ch06:alg:sinkhorn}
  \SetAlgoLined
  \KwIn{State cost $c_{\texttt{tsk}}(\cdot)$ and control cost
  $c_{\texttt{ctr}}(\cdot)$.}
  \KwOut{Optimized trajectory $\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^\star(T)$.}
  Initialize $\alpha_0$, $\lambda$, and $\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{0}(T)$\;
  \rev{Set histograms
  $\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{1}_{T+1}/(T+1)$ and
  $\boldsymbol{m}=\boldsymbol{1}_{M}/M$.}\;
  \While{($k\leq K$) and
    ($\|\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k+1}(T)-\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k}(T)\|
    > \epsilon$)}{
    Build $\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{P}}$ with rotation\;
    Compute $\boldsymbol{C}$ from
    $(\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k}(T),\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{P}})$\;
    Compute $\boldsymbol{W}_\lambda^\star$ from
    \eqref{ch06:eq:OT}\;
    Update $\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k+1}(T)$ by
    \eqref{ch06:eq:sinkhorn}\;
    Update $\alpha_k$ and set $k\leftarrow k+1$\;
  }
  \Return $\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k}(T)$\;
\end{algorithm}

%%===================
\subsubsection{Local optimal transport via Sinkhorn step}
\label{ch06:subsec:sinkhorn}

The local problem in \eqref{ch06:eq:local-problem} remains nonlinear and
non-convex. \rev{Each single-robot update is therefore recast as an
entropy-regularized optimal transport problem and solved by a Sinkhorn-type
iteration \citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn,peyre2019computational,le2023accelerating}.}
The update probes local directions at each waypoint, evaluates their costs in
parallel, and moves the trajectory toward lower-cost regions. The procedure in
Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:sinkhorn} has four stages.
(I) \emph{Initialization}.
At iteration $k$, the current trajectory of robot $i_\ell$ is denoted by
$\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k}(T)$ and is initialized from the
Gaussian-process prior.
(II) \emph{Probing}.
Each waypoint is perturbed along $M$ directions collected in
$\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times 2D}$, where the
directions are generated from a polytope
$\mathcal{P}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2D}$.
\rev{Evaluating the local objective along these perturbations produces the
cost matrix $\boldsymbol{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{(T+1)\times M}$, with one row for
each sample from $0$ through $T$.}
(III) \emph{Weight optimization}.
\begin{revision}
The transport weights are obtained by solving the regularized \emph{optimal
transport problem}
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:OT}
OT_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{m})\triangleq
\underset{\boldsymbol{W}\in U(\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{m})}
{\textbf{min}}
\Big\{
\langle \boldsymbol{W},\boldsymbol{C}\rangle
-\lambda H(\boldsymbol{W})
\Big\},
\end{equation}
where
$\boldsymbol{n}\in\Sigma_{T+1}$ and $\boldsymbol{m}\in\Sigma_M$ are the
histograms over time indices and probing directions, respectively,
$\Sigma_q\triangleq
\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{q}\mid
\sum_{j=1}^{q} a_j=1\}$ is the probability simplex,
$U(\boldsymbol{n},\boldsymbol{m})$ is the set of transport plans with
marginals $\boldsymbol{n}$ and $\boldsymbol{m}$,
$H(\boldsymbol{W})$ is the entropy of the plan, and $\lambda>0$
controls the regularization strength.
\end{revision}
(IV) \emph{Trajectory update}.
Using the optimizer $\boldsymbol{W}_\lambda^\star$ of
\eqref{ch06:eq:OT}, the trajectory is updated by:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:sinkhorn}
\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k+1}(T)
=
\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^{k}(T)
+
\alpha_k\,
\big(\texttt{diag}(\boldsymbol{n})^{-1}\big)
\boldsymbol{W}_\lambda^\star
\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{P}},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_k>0$ is the step size.
The iteration continues until convergence or until the maximum number of
iterations is reached.
After the Sinkhorn update has converged, the optimized trajectory
$\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}^\star(T)$ is added to the current collective set,
and the next robot in the sequence is planned using the updated
preceding set.
In this way, the sequential procedure yields the collective trajectory below:
\[
\Gamma_\kappa\triangleq
\{\mathbf{Z}_{i_1}^\star(T),\cdots,\mathbf{Z}_{i_N}^\star(T)\},
\]
where the overall solution quality depends on the planning sequence
$\kappa$, because early decisions influence the feasible and
cost-effective regions available to later robots.
This interaction between sequence selection and sequential transport
motivates the hybrid search scheme introduced in the next subsection.

Several heuristics improve the numerical behavior of
Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:sinkhorn}. First, randomly rotating the probing
directions in $\boldsymbol{D}_{\mathcal{P}}$ reduces correlation among
waypoint updates. \rev{Second, directional cost averaging uses
$N_{\texttt{s}}>0$ auxiliary samples per direction, producing
$\widetilde{\boldsymbol{D}}_{\mathcal{P}}
\in \mathbb{R}^{(T+1)\times M\times N_{\texttt{s}}\times 2D}$ and reducing
cost-evaluation variance.} Third, annealing $\alpha_k$ and the exploration
radius $\beta_k$ shifts the iteration from exploration to local refinement
\citep{altschuler2017near,le2023accelerating}.

\subsection{Hybrid search for sequence and collective trajectory}
\label{ch06:sec:hybrid}

The sequential local transport scheme reduces problem dimension, but its
performance depends on the planning sequence. Early decisions restrict later
feasible and low-cost regions, so sequence selection and trajectory refinement
must be treated jointly. A hybrid search layer therefore combines discrete
exploration over partial sequences with continuous Sinkhorn-based local
optimization.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Hybrid search framework}
\label{ch06:subsec:search-algorithm}

The search is organized as a rooted tree
$\mathfrak{T}\triangleq (\mathcal{V},\rightarrow)$.
Each node is denoted by
$\nu\triangleq (\mathcal{N}_\nu,\Gamma_\nu)$, where
$\mathcal{N}_\nu\subset\mathcal{N}$ is the set of robots that have
already been planned, and
$\Gamma_\nu\triangleq
\{\mathbf{Z}_{i_1},\cdots,\mathbf{Z}_{i_\ell}\}$ is the associated
partial collective trajectory.
The root node is
$\nu_0\triangleq (\emptyset,\emptyset)$.
A child node is created by appending one additional robot to the current
partial plan and solving the corresponding local trajectory optimization
problem.
As summarized in Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:ss-MPOT}, the search proceeds
through the following four stages.

(I) \emph{Selection}.
At each search round, a subset of $H$ nodes is selected for parallel
expansion, denoted by
$\mathcal{V}_H\triangleq \{\nu_1^\star,\cdots,\nu_H^\star\}$.
These nodes are chosen according to the value function below:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:value}
\chi(\nu)\triangleq
\frac{|\mathcal{N}_\nu|}{N}
-\eta\,\xi(\Gamma_\nu)
+\psi(\nu),
\end{equation}
where the first term measures planning progress, the second term
penalizes the estimated accumulated trajectory cost, and the third term
carries feedback from previous expansions.
Here $\eta>0$ is a weighting parameter,
$\xi(\Gamma_\nu)$ is a surrogate of the partial trajectory cost based on
the same local cost model used in the sequential stage, and
$\psi(\nu)$ is initialized at zero and updated during the search.
Nodes are therefore favored when they correspond to deeper progress,
lower estimated cost, and stronger positive evidence from their
descendants. \rev{The task term in $\xi$ uses the same completion by prior
means as \eqref{ch06:eq:state-cost-sequential}, and $\xi(\varnothing)=0$.}

(II) \emph{Expansion}.
For each selected node $\nu_h^\star$, the robots that are eligible to be
planned next are collected in
$\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}^{\texttt{ext}}\triangleq
\Big(
\bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}}
\mathcal{N}_i^{\overline{\mathcal{E}}}
\Big)
\setminus \mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}$,
which restricts the search to robots that remain compatible with the
current partial plan.
At the root node, the initialization
$\mathcal{N}_{\nu_0}^{\texttt{ext}}\triangleq \mathcal{N}$ is used so
that the search can start from any robot.
\begin{revision}
Once a robot
$i^\star\in\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}^{\texttt{ext}}$ is chosen, the
local Sinkhorn step in Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:sinkhorn} is run from
multiple initial trajectories sampled from the prior, giving the set
\[
\Pi_{i^\star}\triangleq
\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i^\star}^1,\cdots,
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i^\star}^K\},
\]
where $\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i^\star}^k$ is the candidate from initialization $k$.
\end{revision}
Each candidate defines a child node.
This expansion is written below as:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:expand}
\nu_k^+\triangleq
\big(
\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}\cup\{i^\star\},
\Gamma_{\nu_h^\star}\cup\{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i^\star}^k\}
\big),
\end{equation}
where an edge
$(\nu_h^\star,\nu_k^+)\in\rightarrow$
is added for each admissible candidate.
\rev{During expansion, admissibility is checked using the candidate's
endpoints, sampled dynamics, obstacle clearance, and pairwise clearance
against the already planned robots.}
If no feasible candidate trajectory is produced, then the corresponding
branch is not expanded further.

(III) \emph{Back-propagation}.
After expansion, the feedback term in \eqref{ch06:eq:value} is updated
along the ancestor chain of each new node.
For a newly created node $\nu^+$ and any ancestor
$\nu\in\mathcal{V}_{\nu^+}^-$, the update rule is given by:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:propagation}
\psi(\nu)\leftarrow
\psi(\nu)+\psi(\nu^+)\gamma^{d(\nu,\nu^+)},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{V}_{\nu^+}^-\subset\mathcal{V}$ is the set of ancestors
of $\nu^+$, $d(\nu,\nu^+)$ is the path length between $\nu$ and $\nu^+$
in the search tree, and $\gamma\in(0,1)$ is a decay factor.
The value $\psi(\nu^+)$ of each newly accepted node is initialized by a
positive score, so feasible expansions reinforce the priorities of the
ancestor nodes from which they originate.
This mechanism biases later selections toward branches that repeatedly
lead to successful continuations.

(IV) \emph{Termination}.
The cycle of selection, expansion, and back-propagation is repeated
until the prescribed search budget is exhausted.
\begin{revision}
The final ranking includes only nodes with a trajectory for every robot
that pass the verification in Section~\ref{ch06:subsubsec:execute}.
The set of such nodes is
\[
\overline{\mathcal{V}}^\star\triangleq
\{\nu\in\mathcal{V}\mid
\mathcal{N}_\nu=\mathcal{N},\ \texttt{Verify}(\Gamma_\nu)\},
\]
where $\texttt{Verify}$ checks the complete team trajectory.
If this set is empty, the search reports that no verified trajectory was
found within the budget. Otherwise, it selects
\[
\overline{\nu}^\star\triangleq
\operatorname*{argmax}_{\nu\in\overline{\mathcal{V}}^\star}\chi(\nu),
\]
where $\chi$ is the node score in \eqref{ch06:eq:value}, and
$\Gamma_{\overline{\nu}^\star}$ is the associated collective trajectory.
\end{revision}
In this way, the search layer evaluates multiple candidate planning
orders while preserving the continuous local refinement of the
trajectories at each expansion step.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Parallelization and practical improvements}

The hybrid search is naturally parallel. During expansion, local trajectory
optimizations for nodes in $\mathcal{V}_H$ and the resulting child-node
generation can be processed simultaneously; the updates in
\eqref{ch06:eq:propagation} can also be aggregated after each round. Adaptive
expansion improves scalability: early stages benefit from broader exploration,
whereas later stages favor deeper refinement of promising branches. When a node
$\nu$ repeatedly fails, similarity-based pruning removes nearby redundant
nodes,
$\widetilde{\mathcal{V}}_\nu\triangleq
\Big\{
\nu'\in\mathcal{V}\ \Big|\
\|\Gamma_\nu-\Gamma_{\nu'}\|<\delta,\
\mathcal{N}_\nu=\mathcal{N}_{\nu'}
\Big\}$,
where $\|\Gamma_\nu-\Gamma_{\nu'}\|$ denotes a suitable distance
between the two partial collective trajectories and $\delta>0$ is a
pruning threshold.
This rule helps avoid repeated exploration of infeasible or nearly
duplicate branches.
Together, these implementation choices improve scalability, reduce
cascading failures, and accelerate convergence.


%==========
\begin{algorithm}[t!]
\color{blue}
  \caption{\rev{Hybrid search with collaborative optimal transport under bounded-time LTL tasks}}
  \label{ch06:alg:ss-MPOT}
  \SetAlgoLined
  \KwIn{
    Initial state $\mathbf{x}_{\texttt{s}}$,
    goal state $\mathbf{x}_{\texttt{g}}$,
    \rev{task formula $\phi$},
    cost functions $c_{\texttt{ctr}}(\cdot)$ and
    $c_{\texttt{tsk}}(\cdot)$.
  }
  \KwOut{\rev{Verified collective trajectory $\Gamma_{\overline{\nu}^\star}$
  or a failure report.}}

  \rev{Expand $\phi$ on the sample grid and fold constants.}\;
  \If{\rev{the root reduces to false}}{
    \rev{\textbf{return} failure.}\;
  }
  \rev{Construct the finite automaton $\mathcal{A}_\phi$ for verification.}\;
  Initialize $\mathfrak{T}=(\{\nu_0\},\emptyset)$\;

  \While{termination condition is not met \textup{(in parallel)}}{
    \tcc{Selection}
    Select $\mathcal{V}_H$ using \eqref{ch06:eq:value}\;

    \tcc{Expansion}
    \ForEach{$\nu_h^\star\in\mathcal{V}_H$}{
      \eIf{$\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}=\emptyset$}{
        $\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}^{\texttt{ext}}\gets \mathcal{N}$\;
      }{
        $\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}^{\texttt{ext}}\gets
        \big(
        \bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}}
        \mathcal{N}_i^{\overline{\mathcal{E}}}
        \big)
        \setminus
        \mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}$\;
      }

      \If{$\mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}^{\texttt{ext}}\neq\emptyset$}{
        Choose $i^\star\in
        \mathcal{N}_{\nu_h^\star}^{\texttt{ext}}$\;
        \rev{Generate $\Pi_{i^\star}$ by running Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:sinkhorn}
        from the sampled initializations.}\;
        \ForEach{$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i^\star}^k\in \Pi_{i^\star}$}{
          \If{\rev{the local motion and clearance checks pass}}{
            Generate $\nu_k^+$ using \eqref{ch06:eq:expand}\;
            Update $\mathcal{V}$ and $\rightarrow$\;
          }
        }
      }
    }

    \tcc{Back-propagation}
    Update $\psi(\nu)$ using \eqref{ch06:eq:propagation}\;
  }

  \tcc{Termination}
  \rev{$\overline{\mathcal{V}}^\star\gets
  \{\nu\in\mathcal{V}\mid
  \mathcal{N}_\nu=\mathcal{N},\ \texttt{Verify}(\Gamma_\nu)\}$}\;
  \If{\rev{$\overline{\mathcal{V}}^\star=\varnothing$}}{
    \rev{\textbf{return} failure.}\;
  }
  $\overline{\nu}^\star\gets
  \operatorname*{argmax}_{\nu\in\overline{\mathcal{V}}^\star}\chi(\nu)$\;
  \Return $\Gamma_{\overline{\nu}^\star}$\;
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Overall analyses}
\label{ch06:subsec:overall}

The construction yields a pipeline from surrogate robustness computation to
hybrid search over planning orders. The following parts summarize verification,
execution, and dominant computational costs.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Verification and execution}
\label{ch06:subsubsec:execute}

\begin{revision}
For a complete plan $\Gamma_\nu$, $\texttt{Verify}$ checks the endpoints
and sampled dynamics in \eqref{ch06:eq:prob} and the prescribed obstacle and
pairwise clearances at each sample. It then runs $\mathcal{A}_\phi$ with
letters formed from strictly positive signed margins to check positive
exact robustness under \eqref{ch06:eq:stl-robustness}. Every check uses
the optimized trajectories of all robots. The surrogate robustness guides the search and the exact
checks determine which complete plans can be returned.
\end{revision}
For execution, the optimized trajectories
$\{\mathbf{Z}_i^\star(T)\}_{i\in\mathcal{N}}$ can be refined into denser
waypoint sequences, rechecked if needed, and tracked by onboard controllers or
model predictive control.

\begin{revision}
The verification certifies Boolean satisfaction on the sampled trajectory
over the specified finite horizon.
Continuous-time safety between samples and robustness to tracking error
require additional interpolation and error bounds, or direct checks of
the realized trajectory.
\end{revision}
The framework extends to larger fleets and dynamic obstacles. \rev{For large
fleets, robots can be partitioned into subgroups
$\{\mathcal{N}_1,\cdots,\mathcal{N}_G\}$ with local formation constraints,
while a higher-level bounded-time LTL formula is imposed over the groups.} For known or
predictable dynamic obstacles, their motion is incorporated into
\eqref{ch06:eq:state-cost-sequential}; under uncertain motion, conservative
reachable sets or safety buffers can be used instead.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Complexity analysis}
\label{ch06:subsubsec:complexity}

The dominant computational cost of
Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:ss-MPOT} arises from the repeated Sinkhorn updates
performed during node expansion.
\begin{revision}
\rev{Using the complexity estimate for the entropy-regularized optimal
transport step, the cost of one such update is
$\widetilde{O}\!\left(
\tfrac{2D}{\epsilon^2}\cdot
\tfrac{(HN_p(T+1))^2}{\varepsilon^3}
\right)$,
where $D$ is the spatial dimension, $\epsilon>0$ is the convergence
precision of the local transport update, $H$ is the number of parallel
expansions, $N_p$ is the number of sampled trajectories, $T+1$ is the
number of samples per trajectory, and $\varepsilon>0$ is the tolerance used in the
Sinkhorn--Knopp scaling
\citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn,peyre2019computational,le2023accelerating}.}
Evaluating the task-related constraints through the expanded formula
tree contributes a cost of
$O\big(M|\mathcal{T}^\phi|\big)$ per robot, where $M$ is the number of
probing directions and $|\mathcal{T}^\phi|$ is the number of nodes in
the expanded formula tree.
\end{revision}
The remaining bookkeeping operations of the search tree are of lower
order compared with the repeated transport updates and robustness
evaluations.
With $N$ robots, the search tree depth is at most $N$.
In the worst case, the number of explored nodes can still grow rapidly.
In practice, however, pruning and heuristic guidance substantially limit
the effective search.
Empirically, the search often remains close to about $CN$ expansions,
with $C\in[1,3]$ depending on the problem structure and on the degree of
branch pruning.
Thus, although the worst-case complexity remains high, the combination
of parallel expansion, local transport updates, and pruning keeps the
method computationally practical for the scenarios considered here.
