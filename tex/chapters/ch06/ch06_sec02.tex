\section{Reactive Coordination under Dynamic Temporal Logic Tasks}
\label{ch06:sec:ch06_uncertain}

The previous section addressed uncertainty through probabilistic safety and
return guarantees. This section considers dynamic targets, partial motion
forecasts, and online task releases. A task assignment that is feasible at one
instant may become inadequate as target configurations evolve. The coordination
method therefore combines target-trajectory estimation, conformal uncertainty
sets, R-poset task abstraction, and uncertainty-aware assignment, and then embeds
these components in a receding-horizon replanning loop.

\subsection{Task coordination over dynamic temporal tasks}
\label{ch06:sec:problem}


%==============================
\subsubsection{Robots and targets}
\label{ch06:subsec:robots}

Consider a team of $N$ autonomous robots operating in a workspace
$\mathcal{W} \subset \mathbb{R}^{3}$. Let
$\mathcal{N} \triangleq \{1,\cdots,N\}$ denote the robot index set. The
state of robot $n \in \mathcal{N}$ at time $t \geq 0$ is denoted by
$x_{t,n} \in \mathcal{W}$, and the stacked team state is
$x_t \triangleq (x_{t,1},\cdots,x_{t,N})$. The maximum speed of robot
$n$ is denoted by $v_n$. Each robot $n$ can execute actions from the set
$\mathcal{A}_n$. Let
$\mathcal{A} \triangleq \bigcup_{n \in \mathcal{N}} \mathcal{A}_n$.
Let $\mathcal{C}$ denote the set of pre-programmed collaborations. Each
collaboration $C_k \in \mathcal{C}$ consists of a list of actions,
written as $C_k \triangleq [a_1,\cdots,a_{\ell_k}]$, where $\ell_k > 0$
is the number of required actions and $a_{\ell} \in \mathcal{A}$ for
every $\ell \in \{1,\cdots,\ell_k\}$. Each action $a_{\ell} \in C_k$
must be assigned to a capable robot $n$ satisfying
$a_{\ell} \in \mathcal{A}_n$. Each collaboration has a fixed duration
given by the map $\rho : \mathcal{C} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}_{+}$.

The workspace also contains $M$ dynamic targets with unknown
trajectories. Let $\mathcal{M} \triangleq \{1,\cdots,M\}$ denote the
target index set. The position of target $m \in \mathcal{M}$ at time
$t \geq 0$ is modeled as a random variable
$Y_{t,m} \in \mathbb{R}^{2}$, and the joint target state is
$Y_t \triangleq (Y_{t,1},\cdots,Y_{t,M}) \in \mathbb{R}^{2M}$. The
target trajectories are assumed to follow an unknown distribution
$\mathcal{D}$, namely $(Y_0,Y_1,\cdots) \sim \mathcal{D}$. This
distribution is assumed to be independent of the robot-team state $x_t$.
A total of $\bar{K}$ independent trajectory samples
$Y^{(i)} \triangleq (Y_0^{(i)},Y_1^{(i)},\cdots)$ are available. These
samples are partitioned into the calibration set
$D_{\texttt{cal}} \triangleq \{Y^{(1)},\cdots,Y^{(K)}\}$ and the
training set
$D_{\texttt{tra}} \triangleq \{Y^{(K+1)},\cdots,Y^{(\bar{K})}\}$. The
datasets are independently drawn from $\mathcal{D}$
\citep{Lars2023CP,Matthew2024CP}. Perfect observations of target
positions and velocities are assumed to be available throughout
execution. They are denoted by
$Y_{0:t} \triangleq (Y_0,\cdots,Y_t)$ and
$V_{0:t} \triangleq (V_0,\cdots,V_t)$, where
$V_t \triangleq (V_{t,1},\cdots,V_{t,M})$. A centralized estimator aggregates
these measurements. Static task regions and obstacles are modeled as
zero-velocity targets.

%==============================
\subsubsection{Task specification}
\label{ch06:subsec:task-specification}

The task formulas are defined over two classes of atomic propositions. For
robot $n \in \mathcal{N}$ and target $m \in \mathcal{M}$, the proposition
$p_n^m$ is \textit{true} when their distance is below a prescribed threshold.
Let
$\textbf{p} \triangleq
\{p_n^m \mid m \in \mathcal{M},\, n \in \mathcal{N}\}$. For each
collaboration $C_k \in \mathcal{C}$ and target $m \in \mathcal{M}$, the
proposition $c_k^m$ is \textit{true} when collaboration $C_k$ is
executed on target $m$. Let
$\textbf{c} \triangleq
\{c_k^m \mid m \in \mathcal{M},\, C_k \in \mathcal{C}\}$. The complete
set of atomic propositions is
$AP \triangleq \textbf{p} \cup \textbf{c}$.
\begin{revision}
The static task and each response task are sc-LTL formulas over $AP$, as
defined in Section~\ref{ch02:subsec:syntax}. The persistent mission rule is
general LTL:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:reactive-task}
\varphi_{\texttt{rule}} \triangleq \varphi_{\texttt{static}}
\wedge
\bigwedge_{\bar{e} \in \bar{E}}
\Box\bigl(
\varphi_{\texttt{obs}}^{\bar{e}}
\rightarrow
\Diamond \varphi_{\texttt{rep}}^{\bar{e}}
\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\varphi_{\texttt{static}}$ is the offline task, $\bar E$ is the set
of event types, $\varphi_{\texttt{obs}}^{\bar e}$ is the trigger condition,
and $\varphi_{\texttt{rep}}^{\bar e}$ is the corresponding response task.
Let $\bar E_t\subseteq\bar E$ contain the triggers observed at time $t$.
Each trigger releases the finite obligation
$\Diamond\varphi_{\texttt{rep}}^{\bar e}$, evaluated from its release time.
The recurring rule in \eqref{ch06:eq:reactive-task} generally has no finite
good prefix, even though each released obligation is sc-LTL
\citep{baier2008principles,belta2017formal}.
\end{revision}

Thus, given the robot model and the task specification,
the task plan for the robot team is defined by:
\begin{equation}
\color{blue}
\label{ch06:plan}
\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathcal{N}} \triangleq (\tau_1,\tau_2,\cdots,\tau_N),
\end{equation}
where
$\tau_n \triangleq
(t_n^1,a_n^1,m_n^1)(t_n^2,a_n^2,m_n^2)\cdots
(t_n^{K_n},a_n^{K_n},m_n^{K_n})$
is the timed sequence of actions and corresponding targets for robot
$n \in \mathcal{N}$. Here, each action $a_n^k \in \mathcal{A}_n$ is
executed on target $m_n^k \in \mathcal{M}$ at time $t_n^k > 0$, for
$k \in \mathcal{K}_n \triangleq \{1,\cdots,K_n\}$. \rev{The plan in \eqref{ch06:plan} induces a finite word
$w_\tau\in(2^{AP})^*$ of future proposition sets, beginning after the
history already consumed by the task monitor.} \begin{revision}
At time $t$, $\varphi(t)$ is the conjunction of the residual sc-LTL
obligations still pending; $\varphi(0)$ includes the static task and any
initial releases. Each residual retains the progress observed since its
instance's release. Within a finite planning call, the unindexed
$\varphi$ denotes this current formula. Its language
$\mathcal L_{\varphi}\triangleq\{w\in(2^{AP})^*\mid
w\text{ is a good prefix for }\varphi\}$ gives the finite satisfaction
convention $\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathcal N}\models\varphi$ when
$w_\tau\in\mathcal L_\varphi$.
Let $\varphi_1,\cdots,\varphi_L$ be the indexed task instances released by
$t$, including completed instances. For each instance, $T_{\varphi_\ell}$
is the first mission time at which its trace since release is a good prefix.
The notation $|\varphi(t)|\triangleq L$ in the counting expressions below
records this number of released instances, rather than formula length.
The average completion time is
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:avg-makespan}
\overline{T}_{\varphi} \triangleq
\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} T_{\varphi_{\ell}},
\end{equation}
where $L\geq1$ includes the static task. Finite completion concerns these
individual instances. An empty pending set yields an empty assignment while
monitoring continues. Overall termination requires that task releases end
and all pending instances are completed.
\end{revision}

%==============================
\subsubsection{Problem statement}
\label{ch06:subsec:prob-statement}

\rev{Given the current finite obligation formula $\varphi(t)$ and the
observations $\{Y_{0:t},V_{0:t}\}$, synthesize the team plan
$\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathcal N}$ satisfying $\varphi(t)$ under the
finite good-prefix convention, while minimizing the average completion time
in \eqref{ch06:eq:avg-makespan}. New triggers update the planning instance
online; completing one instance does not certify the persistent rule
\eqref{ch06:eq:reactive-task} for all future observations.}

%==============================
\begin{example}
\label{ch06:exp:task}

Consider a fleet of UAVs and ground robots deployed for wildlife
monitoring in a workspace labeled by \texttt{poacher}, \texttt{trap},
and \texttt{animal}. An offline collaborative task is specified by
\eqref{ch06:eq:task}:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:task}
\begin{aligned}
\varphi_{\texttt{1}}
&\triangleq
\varphi_{\texttt{p}-\texttt{s}_1}
\wedge
\varphi_{\texttt{mf}-\texttt{a}}, \\
\varphi_{\texttt{p}-\texttt{s}_1}
&\triangleq
\Diamond \texttt{patrol}_{\texttt{s}_1}, \\
\varphi_{\texttt{mf}-\texttt{a}}
&\triangleq
\Diamond\bigl(
\texttt{monitor}_{\texttt{a}}
\wedge
\neg \texttt{film}_{\texttt{a}}
\wedge
\Diamond \texttt{film}_{\texttt{a}}
\bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\rev{where the formula combines patrol of region $\texttt{s}_1$ with
sequential monitoring and filming of the antelope.} Ground robots must also avoid
obstacles in the workspace. In addition, the reactive rule
$\texttt{poacher} \rightarrow \Diamond \texttt{arrest}$ releases an
arrest task when a poacher is detected, whereas the rule
$\texttt{trap} \wedge \texttt{animal} \rightarrow
\Diamond \texttt{rescue}$ releases a rescue task when a trapped animal
is observed.
\end{example}

\subsection{Trajectory estimation of dynamic targets}
\label{ch06:subsec:trajectory}

The coordination problem uses target-motion forecasts, not only instantaneous
observations. The following parts define the trajectory predictor and the
conformal prediction regions used for uncertainty quantification.

\subsubsection{Trajectory predictor}
\label{ch06:subsec:traj-predictor}

Given a task horizon $T_{\varphi}$ and the observation history
$Y_{0:t}$, an independent trajectory predictor is trained for each
target using the training dataset $D_{\texttt{tra}}$. For target
$m \in \mathcal{M}$, let
$D_{\texttt{tra}}^m \subset D_{\texttt{tra}}$ denote the subset of
training trajectories associated with target $m$. For each
$Y^{(i)} \in D_{\texttt{tra}}^m$, write
$Y_{0:T_{\varphi},m}^{(i)} \triangleq
(Y_{0,m}^{(i)},\cdots,Y_{t,m}^{(i)},Y_{t+1,m}^{(i)},\cdots,
Y_{T_{\varphi},m}^{(i)})$.
The predictor for target $m$ is a map
$\Upsilon_m : \mathbb{R}^{2(t+1)} \rightarrow
\mathbb{R}^{2(T_{\varphi}-t)}$ that estimates the future target states
over the horizon
$\mathcal{H}_t \triangleq \{t+1,\cdots,T_{\varphi}\}$. The predicted
trajectory of target $m$ is therefore
$\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t,m} \triangleq \Upsilon_m(Y_{0:t,m})$,
with
$\Upsilon_m(Y_{0:t,m}) \triangleq
(\widehat{Y}_{t+1 \mid t,m},\cdots,\widehat{Y}_{T_{\varphi} \mid t,m})$.
Let
$\mathbf{\Upsilon} \triangleq (\Upsilon_1,\cdots,\Upsilon_M)$ denote the
collection of all target-wise predictors.
Any suitable trajectory predictor may be used, including LSTMs
\citep{Hochreiter1997LSTM}, recurrent neural networks~\citep{rnn}, and gated
recurrent units~\citep{gru}. Here, one LSTM is adopted per target, with the
training objective:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:traj-loss}
\min_{\Upsilon_m}
\frac{1}{|D_{\texttt{tra}}^m|}
\sum_{i=1}^{|D_{\texttt{tra}}^m|}
\left\|
Y_{\mathcal{H}_t,m}^{(i)} -
\Upsilon_m(Y_{0:t,m}^{(i)})
\right\|^2,
\end{equation}
where $Y_{\mathcal{H}_t,m}^{(i)}$ is the future segment of the
trajectory sample $Y_{0:T_{\varphi},m}^{(i)}$. The resulting
target-wise predictions are stacked as
$\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t} \triangleq
(\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t,1},\cdots,\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t,M})$.
\begin{revision}
The measured maximum speed
$\widehat v_m(t)\triangleq\max_{\varsigma\le t}|V_{\varsigma,m}|$
is a diagnostic, not a bound on future motion. The planning bound
$v_m^\ast$ is a separately specified valid upper bound on target speed
throughout the forecast and pursuit interval. If such a bound is unavailable,
the delay expression below is only a heuristic estimate and its guarantee
is not asserted.
\end{revision}

\subsubsection{Conformal prediction regions}
\label{ch06:subsec:cpr}

\rev{Conformal prediction constructs uncertainty regions around the nominal
forecasts and provides finite-sample coverage guarantees
\citep{Angelopoulos2021CP}. Following \citet{Lars2023CP}, the regions are built
\rev{separately for each target; separate construction does not imply
statistical independence of their coverage events.}}
Given observations $Y_{0:t}$ at time $t$, the predictors
$\mathbf{\Upsilon}$ generate the nominal forecasts
$\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t}$. For each target
$m \in \mathcal{M}$ and each failure probability
$\delta \in (0,1)$, define
$G_{\mathcal{H}_t,m} \triangleq
(G_{t+1 \mid t,m},\cdots,G_{T_{\varphi} \mid t,m})$. The
coverage guarantee is given by \eqref{ch06:eq:cpg}:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:cpg}
\Pr\Big(
\|Y_{h,m} - \widehat{Y}_{h \mid t,m}\|
\leq G_{h \mid t,m},
\ \forall h \in \mathcal{H}_t
\Big) \geq 1 - \delta,
\end{equation}
\rev{where $G_{h\mid t,m}$ is the radius for target $m$ at step $h$.
The split-conformal guarantee assumes exchangeability of the calibration
and test trajectories and a predictor fixed before calibration. It is
marginal coverage at a fixed forecast origin, not conditional coverage for
every observation history. The union bound over forecast steps gives
\eqref{ch06:eq:cpg}; repeated online origins require their own failure-budget
allocation for a mission-wide guarantee.}
To compute these radii, define the nonconformity score by
\eqref{ch06:eq:ncs}:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:ncs}
R_{h \mid t,m}^{(i)} \triangleq
\left\|
Y_{h,m}^{(i)} - \widehat{Y}_{h \mid t,m}^{(i)}
\right\|,
\end{equation}
where $Y^{(i)} \in D_{\texttt{cal}}^m$ is a calibration trajectory and
$D_{\texttt{cal}}^m$ is the calibration dataset associated with target
$m$. For every $Y^{(i)} \in D_{\texttt{cal}}^m$, the predictor produces
$\widehat{Y}_{h \mid t,m}^{(i)}$, and the score
$R_{h \mid t,m}^{(i)}$ is then evaluated using
\eqref{ch06:eq:ncs}. Let
$\bar{\delta} \triangleq \delta/(T_{\varphi}-t)$, and augment the score
set by
$R_{h \mid t,m}^{(|D_{\texttt{cal}}^m|+1)} \triangleq \infty$.
After sorting the resulting scores in non-decreasing order, let
$p \triangleq
\left\lceil
(|D_{\texttt{cal}}^m|+1)(1-\bar{\delta})
\right\rceil$.
\rev{The bound $G_{h\mid t,m}$ is the $p$-th smallest score.
For the guarantee below, nominal event estimates are rounded up to this
forecast grid. Events beyond the grid require a new forecast and are not
covered by extrapolating the old radii.}
Finally, the prediction regions for all targets are collected as
$G_{\mathcal{H}_t} \triangleq
(G_{\mathcal{H}_t,1},\cdots,G_{\mathcal{H}_t,M})$.

%=======================================
\setlength{\textfloatsep}{5pt}
\begin{algorithm}[t]
    \caption{Offline construction of conformal prediction regions}
    \label{ch06:alg:cp}
    \SetKwInOut{Input}{Input}
    \SetKwInOut{Output}{Output}
    \Input{Failure probability $\delta$, task horizon $T_{\varphi}$,
      calibration dataset $D_{\texttt{cal}}^m$.}
    \Output{Prediction radii $G_{h \mid t,m}$.}
    \For{$t$ from $0$ to $T_{\varphi}-1$}{
        $\bar{\delta} \gets \delta/(T_{\varphi}-t)$;\\
        $p \gets
        \left\lceil
        (|D_{\texttt{cal}}^m|+1)(1-\bar{\delta})
        \right\rceil$;\\
        \For{$h$ from $t+1$ to $T_{\varphi}$}{
            Obtain $\widehat{Y}_{h \mid t,m}^{(i)}$ for each
            $Y^{(i)} \in D_{\texttt{cal}}^m$;\\
            Compute $R_{h \mid t,m}^{(i)}$ by
            \eqref{ch06:eq:ncs};\\
            $R_{h \mid t,m}^{(|D_{\texttt{cal}}^m|+1)}
            \gets \infty$;\\
            Sort $R_{h \mid t,m}^{(i)}$ in non-decreasing order;\\
            $G_{h \mid t,m} \gets R_{h \mid t,m}^{(p)}$;\\
        }
    }
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{R-posets for task formulas}
\label{ch06:subsec:poset}

R-posets were introduced in Chapter~\ref{chap:top-down}, especially
Section~\ref{ch04:subsec:task-decompose-posets}, as compact abstractions of
team-level temporal constraints. The construction is reused here, so only the
needed ingredients are recalled.
\begin{revision}
Here $\varphi$ denotes the current finite obligation formula $\varphi(t)$.
Given this sc-LTL task, use the good-prefix construction of
Section~\ref{ch02:subsec:syntax} to obtain the Nondeterministic B\"uchi automaton
$\mathcal{A}_{\varphi} \triangleq (S,\Sigma,\delta,S_0,S_F)$, where
$S$ is the state set, $\Sigma \triangleq 2^{AP}$ is the alphabet,
$\delta : S \times \Sigma \rightarrow 2^{S}$ is the transition
relation, and $S_0, S_F \subseteq S$ are the initial and accepting
state sets. Accepting states are absorbing, and finite acceptance recognizes
exactly the good prefixes of $\varphi$. From a finite prefix ending in $S_F$,
the decomposition procedure in Definition~\ref{ch04:def:subtasks} extracts
the indexed subtasks $\Omega_{\varphi}$ and their temporal relations.
\end{revision}
\rev{The subsequent relaxation proposes candidate partial constraints
in place of the seed order. Complete schedules must still pass the original
automaton check; the partial constraints alone do not certify correctness.}
Recall that an R-poset associated with $\varphi$ is the triple:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:r-poset}
P_{\varphi} \triangleq
(\Omega_{\varphi},\preceq_{\varphi},\neq_{\varphi}),
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_{\varphi}$ is the subtask set,
$\preceq_{\varphi} \subseteq
\Omega_{\varphi} \times \Omega_{\varphi}$ is the precedence relation,
and $\neq_{\varphi} \subseteq 2^{\Omega_{\varphi}}$ is the
mutual-exclusion relation. The relation
$(\omega_1,\omega_2) \in \preceq_{\varphi}$ means that
$\omega_2$ cannot start before $\omega_1$ starts. The relation
$\widehat{\Omega} \in \neq_{\varphi}$ for some
$\widehat{\Omega} \subseteq \Omega_{\varphi}$ means that the subtasks in
$\widehat{\Omega}$ cannot be executed simultaneously.


The definition of $P_{\varphi}$ specializes
Definition~\ref{ch04:def:partial}, Definition~\ref{ch04:def:poset}, and
\eqref{ch04:eq:poset} to the task model in
Section~\ref{ch06:sec:problem}. It is less restrictive than the full automaton
run because it retains only essential temporal dependencies, but it remains
task-consistent: by Definition~\ref{ch04:def:language-poset},
\eqref{ch04:eq:poset-language}, and
Lemma~\ref{ch04:lemma:accepting-poset}, every accepting R-poset returned by
Algorithm~\ref{ch04:alg:compute-poset} satisfies
$L(P_{\varphi}) \subseteq \mathcal{L}_{\varphi}$. In practice,
$P_{\varphi}$ is computed by
$\texttt{compute\_poset}(\cdot)$ and retains the precedence and
synchronization relations needed for assignment and execution.

\subsection{Uncertainty-aware task assignment}
\label{ch06:subsec:assignment}

Given $(\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t},G_{\mathcal{H}_t})$ and
$P_{\varphi}=(\Omega_{\varphi},\preceq_{\varphi},\neq_{\varphi})$, the
objective is to assign all subtasks to the team while respecting
$\preceq_{\varphi}$ and $\neq_{\varphi}$ and minimizing
$\overline{T}_{\varphi}$. This extends the assignment model of
Chapter~\ref{chap:top-down} to completion times driven by predicted target
motion and uncertainty.

\subsubsection{CP-based Monte Carlo tree search}
\label{ch06:sec:cp-mcts}

A centralized Monte Carlo Tree Search addresses the combinatorial assignment
problem. Unlike the deterministic assignment procedure in
Chapter~\ref{chap:top-down}, the method evaluates partial plans using predicted
target trajectories and conformal uncertainty regions. The resulting CP-based
MCTS, summarized in Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:cp-mcts}, alternates selection,
expansion, simulation, and backpropagation under a fixed time budget. Because
expansion can generate many child nodes, a prediction-based screening metric
retains only promising nodes for simulation.

(I) \emph{Selection and Expansion}.
Each node of the search tree represents a partial assignment of
subtasks, written as
$\nu \triangleq (\tau_1,\cdots,\tau_N)$, where $\tau_n$ is the ordered
sequence of subtasks currently assigned to robot $n \in \mathcal{N}$.
During selection, the Upper Confidence Bound applied to Trees
criterion~\citep{2006Bandit} is used. The UCT score of the $i$-th child
is
$\overline{\xi}_i + Q\sqrt{\ln B/b_i}$, where $\overline{\xi}_i$ is the
estimated value of that child, $b_i$ is its visit count, $B$ is the
visit count of the current node, and $Q > 0$ balances exploration and
exploitation. Starting from the root, the child with the largest UCT
score is selected recursively until a leaf node is reached.

During expansion, unless a terminal node with a complete assignment has
been reached, child nodes are generated by assigning one admissible
next subtask. Let
$\Omega_{\nu} \triangleq
\bigcup_{n \in \mathcal{N}}\{\omega \mid \omega \in \tau_n\}$
denote the set of subtasks already assigned at node $\nu$, and let
$\Omega_{\nu}^{-} \triangleq \Omega_{\varphi}\setminus\Omega_{\nu}$
denote the set of unassigned subtasks. The admissible next-subtask set
is defined by:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:admissible}
\Omega_{\nu}^{\texttt{a}} \triangleq
\{\omega \in \Omega_{\nu}^{-} \mid
\operatorname{Pre}(\omega)\subseteq \Omega_{\nu}\},
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Pre}(\omega)$ is the set of predecessors of
$\omega$ in the R-poset. Equation~\eqref{ch06:eq:admissible} is the
same structural feasibility condition used in Chapter~\ref{chap:top-down}.
Once a subtask $\omega \in \Omega_{\nu}^{\texttt{a}}$ is selected, a
child node $\nu^{+}$ is created by assigning $\omega$ to a feasible
robot group $\mathcal{I}_{\omega}$ and appending it to the local plan
of each robot in that group. For the resulting child node, define the
set of key subtasks by:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:key-subtasks}
\Omega_{\nu^{+}}^{\texttt{s}} \triangleq
\Bigl\{
\omega \in \Omega_{\nu^{+}}
\;\Bigm|\;
\exists n \in \mathcal{N} \text{ such that } \omega=\tau_n[0],
\ \omega \notin \tau_{n'}[1:] \text{ for all } n' \in \mathcal{N}
\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_{\nu^{+}}^{\texttt{s}}$ contains the subtasks predicted to
be executed first across the robots. Stepwise simulation based on the
nominal trajectories $\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t}$ then yields a
predicted completion time $\widehat{T}_{\omega}$ for each assigned
subtask $\omega \in \Omega_{\nu^{+}}$.

%=======================================
\setlength{\textfloatsep}{5pt}
\begin{algorithm}
\color{blue}[!t]
    \caption{CP-based MCTS for uncertainty-aware task assignment}
    \label{ch06:alg:cp-mcts}
    \SetKwInOut{Input}{Input}
    \SetKwInOut{Output}{Output}
    \Input{Robots $\mathcal{N}$, current team state $x_t$, R-poset
    $P_{\varphi}$, duration map $\rho$, time budget $t_b$, target
    estimates $\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t}, G_{\mathcal{H}_t}$,
    \rev{scenario law $\mathbb Q_t$, and confidence level $\alpha\in(0,1)$.}}
    \Output{\rev{A verified plan $\Pi_{\texttt c}^\star$ and risk value
    $\eta_{\texttt c}^\star$, or failure.}}
    \rev{Initialize $\nu_0$, $\Pi_{\texttt c}^\star\leftarrow\varnothing$,
    and $\eta_{\texttt c}^\star\leftarrow\infty$.}\;
    \While{$\mathrm{time} < t_b$}{
        $\nu \leftarrow \texttt{Selection}(\nu_0)$\;
        $\{\nu_{+}\} \leftarrow
        \texttt{Expansion}(\nu,P_{\varphi})$\;
        Filter the candidate nodes by $\zeta$ in
        \eqref{ch06:eq:metric} and obtain
        $\{\nu_{\texttt{s}}\}$\;
        \ForEach{$\nu_{\texttt{s}} \in \{\nu_{\texttt{s}}\}$}{
            $\Pi_{\texttt{c}} \leftarrow
            \texttt{Rollout}(\nu_{\texttt{s}},P_{\varphi},\rho,
            \widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t},x_t)$\;
            $\eta_{\texttt{c}} \leftarrow
            \texttt{Eval}(\Pi_{\texttt{c}},P_{\varphi},\rho,
            \widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t},G_{\mathcal{H}_t},x_t)$\;
            \If{\rev{$\eta_{\texttt c}<\eta_{\texttt c}^\star$ and the complete
            plan passes the original-automaton trace check}}{
                $\eta_{\texttt{c}}^\star \leftarrow
                \eta_{\texttt{c}}$\;
                $\Pi_{\texttt{c}}^\star \leftarrow
                \Pi_{\texttt{c}}$\;
            }
            $\xi_{\texttt{c}} \leftarrow
            2-\eta_{\texttt{c}}/\eta_{\texttt{c}}^\star$\;
            \texttt{Backpropagate}($\nu_{\texttt{s}},\xi_{\texttt{c}}$)\;
        }
    }
    \rev{Return failure if no verified complete plan was retained.}\;
\end{algorithm}
%=======================================


(II) \emph{Simulation}.
The simulation stage completes the partial assignment by rollout, evaluates the
resulting plan under samples from the prediction regions, and computes a risk
metric from the induced average-makespan distribution. Rollout recursively
chooses admissible subtasks from \eqref{ch06:eq:admissible} and assigns them to
feasible robot groups. An $\epsilon$-randomized rule selects a random feasible
group with probability $\epsilon$ and the earliest-start group otherwise. \begin{revision}
The complete plan is evaluated using $z$ joint trajectory scenarios drawn
from an explicitly supplied probability law $\mathbb Q_t$.
The law describes temporal and inter-target dependence and is an input to
$\texttt{Rollout}$ and $\texttt{Eval}$, with this argument suppressed below.
Conformal regions alone do not define a distribution for these scenarios.
The resulting CVaR is a risk measure under $\mathbb Q_t$; marginal coverage
of the regions does not calibrate its tail probabilities.

For an integrable completion-time cost $Z=\mathbf{\overline T}_{\texttt c}$
and confidence level $\alpha\in(0,1)$, define
$\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha\triangleq
\inf\{r\in\mathbb R\mid\mathbb Q_t(Z\le r)\ge\alpha\}$.
The upper-tail CVaR is
\[
\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha\triangleq
\min_{r\in\mathbb R}
\left\{r+\frac{\mathbb E_{\mathbb Q_t}[(Z-r)_+]}{1-\alpha}\right\},
\]
where $(z)_+\triangleq\max\{z,0\}$ and $\alpha$ is a confidence level.
This definition handles probability mass at the quantile; it is not in
general the conditional mean given $Z\ge\mathrm{VaR}_\alpha$
\citep{rockafellar2002cvar}.
For scenarios $Z_1,\cdots,Z_z$, the expectation is replaced by their
empirical mean in the same minimization. The endpoint $\alpha=1$ is not
used in this finite-sample definition.
The plan metric is $\eta_{\texttt c}\triangleq
\mathrm{CVaR}_\alpha(\Pi_{\texttt c})$.
The best verified complete plan and its metric are maintained during search.
Verification includes its actual proposition trace, as required by
Section~\ref{ch04:subsubsec:task-decompose}.
\end{revision}

(III) \emph{Backpropagation}.
To support the tree search, define the normalized performance score
$\xi_{\texttt{c}} \triangleq
2-\frac{\eta_{\texttt{c}}}{\eta_{\texttt{c}}^\star}$,
which permits comparison of nodes across different branches. During
backpropagation, the value estimates and visit counts are propagated
from the selected node back to the root. The screening metric used
after expansion is defined below:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:metric}
\zeta_{\nu^{+}} \triangleq
\frac{
\sum_{i=1}^{|\Omega_{\nu^{+}}|}\widehat{T}_{\omega_i}
+
\sum_{j=1}^{|\Omega_{\nu^{+}}^{\texttt{s}}|}
\widehat{T}_{\omega_j}^{\texttt{e}}
}{
|\Omega_{\nu^{+}}|
},
\end{equation}
where
$\widehat{T}_{\omega_j}^{\texttt{e}} \triangleq
G_{\widehat{T}_{\omega_j},m}/
(\min_{n \in \mathcal{I}_{\omega_j}} \{v_n - v_m^{\ast}\})$ is the
uncertainty-induced delay estimate. Equation~\eqref{ch06:eq:metric}
approximates the average completion time of the currently assigned
subtasks after uncertainty adjustment. After expansion, the child node
with smallest $\zeta_{\nu^{+}}$ for each candidate next subtask is sent
to simulation, and the retained nodes form the filtered set
$\{\nu_{\texttt{s}}\}$. If
$\min_{n \in \mathcal{I}_{\omega}} v_n \leq v_m^{\ast}$
for some key subtask $\omega \in \Omega_{\nu^{+}}^{\texttt{s}}$, the
assigned robots may fail to close the distance to the target. In that
case, the node is treated as infeasible by setting
$\widehat{T}_{\omega} = \infty$, which yields
$\zeta_{\nu^{+}} = \infty$. Such nodes are excluded before simulation.
For comparison, the no-trajectory-prediction baseline NTP treats all targets
as stationary, while the no-uncertainty baseline NU uses
$\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t}$ but ignores $G_{\mathcal{H}_t}$. \rev{The CVaR criterion incorporates the chosen scenario law and
forecasts; prediction radii enter the geometric screening separately.}


\begin{revision}
\begin{lemma}
\label{ch06:lemma:key-bound}
Fix a forecast origin $t$ and suppose \eqref{ch06:eq:cpg} holds for each
of the $M$ targets over the full forecast grid. Suppose the selected nominal
times lie in that grid, target speeds are bounded by $v_m^\ast$, and the
assigned robots can execute unobstructed feasible pursuit with closing speed
at least $\min_{n\in\mathcal I_\omega}v_n-v_m^\ast>0$.
If the task-duration model remains valid during pursuit, then
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:key-bound}
\Pr\left(
T_\omega\le\widehat T_\omega+
\frac{G_{\widehat T_\omega,m}}
{\min_{n\in\mathcal I_\omega}v_n-v_m^\ast},\quad
\forall\omega\in\Omega_{\nu^+}^{\texttt s}
\right)\ge\max\{0,1-M\delta\},
\end{equation}
where $m$ is the target of subtask $\omega$ and the radius is evaluated on
the forecast grid. The guarantee is marginal at this forecast origin.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each target let $E_m$ be its simultaneous forecast-coverage event in
\eqref{ch06:eq:cpg}. The union bound gives
$\Pr(\bigcap_{m=1}^M E_m)\ge\max\{0,1-M\delta\}$ without independence.
On this event every selected target and grid time is covered, including
choices made from the same forecasts. Repeated subtasks for one target use
the same horizon event. At the nominal arrival time the remaining distance
is at most the corresponding radius. The assumed positive closing speed
bounds the additional pursuit time by the displayed ratio. The stated
duration assumption then transfers this arrival bound to completion times.
\end{proof}
\end{revision}

\begin{algorithm}
\color{blue}[!t]
    \caption{Online reactive and dynamic task assignment}
    \label{ch06:alg:ta}
    \SetKwInOut{Input}{Input}
    \SetKwInOut{Output}{Output}
    \Input{Robots $\mathcal{N}$, task formula $\varphi(t)$, duration
    map $\rho$, trajectory predictors $\mathbf{\Upsilon}$, prediction
    regions $G_{\mathcal{H}_t}$, initial team state $x_0$, and
    observations $\{Y_{0:t},V_{0:t}\}$, \rev{scenario law $\mathbb Q_t$, and confidence level $\alpha$.}}
    \Output{Assignment $\Pi^{\star}$.}
    \rev{Initialize $t\leftarrow0$ and $\Omega_\iota\leftarrow\emptyset$.}\;
    \rev{Progress the static task and initial releases using the initial
    propositions to obtain $\varphi(0)$.}\;
    \If{\rev{no obligation is pending}}{
        \rev{Set $\Pi^\star=\emptyset$ and $\eta^\star=\overline T_\varphi$.}\;
    }
    \Else{
        \rev{$P_\varphi\leftarrow\texttt{compute\_poset}(\varphi(0))$.}\;
        Initialize
        $\Pi^{\star},\eta^{\star} \leftarrow
        \texttt{CP-MCTS}(P_{\varphi},\rho,
        \widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_0},G_{\mathcal{H}_0},x_0)$\;
    }
    \While{the execution is not terminated}{
        \rev{Advance $t\leftarrow t+1$.}\;
        \rev{Each robot $n\in\mathcal N$ follows its assigned local plan,
        or waits if its assignment is empty.}\;
        Sense the team state $x_t$ and the target observation $Y_t$\;
        Obtain the updated prediction
        $\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t}$\;
        \rev{Progress earlier obligations and new releases using the observed propositions.}\;
        Update $\varphi(t)$ by \eqref{ch06:eq:task-update}\;
        \rev{Record newly completed subtasks and formula-instance times,
        and remove completed work from the residual assignment.}\;
        \rev{$\Omega_\iota\leftarrow\Omega_\iota\cup\Omega_{\texttt c}(t)$.}\;
        \If{\rev{no obligation is pending}}{
            \rev{Set $\Pi^\star=\emptyset$ and $\eta^\star=\overline T_\varphi$; continue monitoring.}\;
            \rev{\textbf{continue}}\;
        }
        Update $P_{\varphi}$ by
        $\texttt{compute\_poset}(\varphi(t))$\;
        \If{\rev{$\bar E_t \neq \emptyset$}}{
            $\Pi^{\star},\eta^{\star} \leftarrow
            \texttt{CP-MCTS}(P_{\varphi},\rho,
            \widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t},G_{\mathcal{H}_t},x_t)$\;
        }
        \Else{
            \rev{Retain the baseline until an adoption or completion update.}\;
            \If{$\Omega_{\texttt{c}}(t) \neq \emptyset$}{
                \rev{Refresh $\eta^\star$ by \eqref{ch06:eq:update_eta}.}\;
            }
            $\eta_t^{\star} \leftarrow
            \texttt{Eval}(\Pi^{\star},P_{\varphi},\rho,
            \widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t},G_{\mathcal{H}_t},x_t)$\;
            \If{$\eta_t^{\star} > (1+\gamma)\eta^{\star}$}{
                $\widehat{\Pi}_t,\widehat{\eta}_t \leftarrow
                \texttt{CP-MCTS}(P_{\varphi},\rho,
                \widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t},G_{\mathcal{H}_t},x_t)$\;
                \If{$\widehat{\eta}_t < \eta_t^{\star}$}{
                    $\Pi^{\star} \leftarrow \widehat{\Pi}_t$\;
                    $\eta^{\star} \leftarrow \widehat{\eta}_t$\;
                }
            }
        }
    }
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Overall analyses}
\label{ch06:subsec:execution}

\subsubsection{Online execution and adaptation}

The assignment method is embedded in receding-horizon execution. As in
Chapter~\ref{chap:top-down}, the current plan is executed non-preemptively,
and replanning is triggered only by new task information or sufficient
assignment-quality degradation. Algorithm~\ref{ch06:alg:ta} has two stages:
initial planning and online adaptation. \rev{A failed planning call retains a still-valid
existing continuation or reports failure; it is not treated as a completed
assignment.} Robots execute local plans by moving to
assigned targets and performing the required actions. Multi-robot actions wait
for all collaborators, while non-collaborative actions proceed independently.
At each time step $t>0$, the team state $x_t$ and the target
observations $Y_t$ are updated, and new target predictions
$\widehat{Y}_{\mathcal{H}_t}$ are generated from $Y_{0:t}$ by the
predictors $\mathbf{\Upsilon}$.
\begin{revision}
The finite obligation formula is updated whenever new reactive tasks are released:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:task-update}
\varphi(t) \triangleq \varphi(t^{-}) \wedge \varphi_{\texttt{r}}(t),
\end{equation}
where $\varphi(t^-)$ is the progressed residual of earlier obligations.
The formula $\varphi_{\texttt r}(t)$ is the residual of
$\bigwedge_{\bar e\in\bar E_t}\Diamond\varphi_{\texttt{rep}}^{\bar e}$
after reading the propositions observed at release time $t$.
The empty conjunction is $\top$.
Each new obligation is evaluated from its release time; the recorded progress
of earlier instances is retained when $P_\varphi$ is updated.
\end{revision}

Replanning is triggered in either of two situations. \rev{The first is the
release of a new reactive task, namely $\bar E_t\neq\emptyset$.} The second is a significant
degradation in the quality of the current plan, namely
$\eta_t^{\star} > (1+\gamma)\eta^{\star}$, where
$\gamma \in (0,1)$ is the replanning threshold. The quantity
$\eta_t^{\star}$ is obtained from $\texttt{Eval}(\cdot)$ using the
latest state and target information. \begin{revision}
A completion changes the residual plan rather than the number of released
formula instances. The baseline metric is refreshed by the same evaluator:
\begin{equation}
\label{ch06:eq:update_eta}
\eta_+^\star\triangleq
\texttt{Eval}(\Pi_{\mathrm{rem}}^\star,P_{\varphi(t)},\rho,
\widehat Y_{\mathcal H_t},G_{\mathcal H_t},x_t),
\end{equation}
where $\Pi_{\mathrm{rem}}^\star$ is the residual assignment after confirmed
completion and the evaluation uses $\mathbb Q_t$.
Completed formula instances contribute their observed completion times;
pending instances contribute their scenario completion times, with the same
released-instance denominator $L$ as in \eqref{ch06:eq:avg-makespan}.
The subtask record $\Omega_\iota$ is used only for execution bookkeeping.
No formula count is divided by a subtask count, and the average absolute
completion-time objective is not decremented once per clock tick.
\end{revision}
Whenever replanning is activated, CP-MCTS produces a candidate plan
$\widehat{\Pi}_t$ with associated metric $\widehat{\eta}_t$. The new
plan is adopted only if it improves upon the current one, that is, if
$\widehat{\eta}_t < \eta_t^{\star}$. \rev{Otherwise, the current residual plan is retained. Metric changes
from completed instances or updated predictions need not be monotone;
they are evaluated in the same objective units.} This procedure
is repeated until the mission terminates.

\subsubsection{Complexity and generalization}

\begin{revision}
The $\mathcal O(J^2)$ relation bookkeeping for a seed with $J$ subtasks
is not the complexity of constructing all R-posets. Simple-path enumeration,
relaxation tests, and final automaton checks add their own, potentially
exponential, costs. For a fixed subtask set and $C$ candidate robot groups,
a crude bound on ordered group assignments is $J!C^J$; in general
$C\le2^N-1$, rather than $C=N$ as in single-robot assignment.
A greedy rollout tests up to $JC$ group choices, in addition to its
navigation and duration evaluations. Simulating $z$ scenarios at time step
$\Delta t$ has the stated model-evaluation cost
$\mathcal O(zT(J+N)/\Delta t)$ when each step uses linear work in the
represented robots and subtasks. Empirical CVaR evaluation adds
$\mathcal O(z\log z)$ sorting work, and each retained complete schedule
requires an automaton trace check. These are component costs; they do not
imply a polynomial total search or general completeness.
\end{revision}
Two extensions are immediate. For robot failures, CP-MCTS can be restarted
after removing failed robots, retaining completed subtasks, and returning
interrupted subtasks to the pending set. For task priorities, weights
$\alpha_{\ell}$ replace the average makespan by
$\widetilde{T}_{\varphi} \triangleq
\sum_{\ell=1}^{|\varphi(t)|} \alpha_{\ell} T_{\varphi_{\ell}}$.
